The Best VPNs Actually Worth Paying For in 2026

Tomas Berg · Updated Sep 26, 2026 · 11 min read

Most "best VPN" lists online are really just affiliate link dumps dressed up as advice. This one isn't going to pretend every VPN on the market is worth your money. Some are. Most aren't. Here's a straight breakdown of which ones actually earn a spot in your monthly subscriptions in 2026, and why.

Why a VPN still matters in 2026

A VPN, short for virtual private network, routes your internet traffic through an encrypted tunnel to a server somewhere else before it reaches the open internet. Two things happen when you do this: your internet provider and anyone snooping on your network can no longer see what you're doing, and the websites you visit see the VPN server's location instead of yours.

That second part is why VPNs became popular for streaming, but the privacy angle has become more relevant over the last few years. Internet providers in several countries are allowed to log and sell browsing data. Public Wi-Fi at airports, cafes, and hotels remains a genuinely easy target for anyone running basic packet-sniffing tools. And more people are working remotely from networks they don't control, which makes a VPN less of a privacy luxury and more of a basic precaution, similar to using a password manager.

None of this means you need the most expensive VPN on the market. It means you need one that actually does what it claims, doesn't quietly log your activity, and doesn't slow your connection to a crawl.

What actually separates a good VPN from a bad one

Before getting into specific recommendations, it's worth knowing what actually matters when comparing VPNs, since most marketing pages lean on the same vague buzzwords.

  • A verified no-logs policy. Anyone can claim they don't log your activity. The ones worth trusting have had that claim independently audited by a third-party security firm.
  • Where the company is legally based. This determines what government data requests they can legally be forced to comply with.
  • Real-world speed, not just marketing numbers. A VPN that constantly disconnects or throttles your connection isn't saving you anything, even if it's cheap.
  • Server spread. More server locations generally means better speeds and more reliable access to region-locked content.
  • Simultaneous device support. Most households now have phones, laptops, tablets, and smart TVs that all need coverage under one plan.
Encryption strength and server location both affect how private and how fast your connection actually is.

1. NordVPN — best all-around pick

NordVPN has built a reputation as the safe default choice, and for most people, that's a fair place to land. It covers the basics well: a large, well-distributed server network, an independently audited no-logs policy, and consistently solid speeds for everyday browsing and streaming.

2. ExpressVPN — best for streaming and travel

ExpressVPN tends to be the more dependable option specifically when it comes to unblocking streaming libraries and staying connected while traveling internationally. It's priced a bit higher than some competitors, but the reliability is the trade-off you're paying for.

3. ProtonVPN — best for privacy-focused users

Built by the same team behind the encrypted email service Proton Mail, ProtonVPN leans heavily into its privacy-first reputation. It's based in Switzerland, which has notably strong privacy laws, and it offers a genuinely usable free tier, not just a bait-and-switch trial.

4. Surfshark — best budget pick

Surfshark is consistently one of the cheaper options on this list, and unlike most budget VPNs, it doesn't feel like you're sacrificing much to get that price. Its unlimited simultaneous device connections make it a strong choice for families or anyone covering several devices under one plan.

5. Mullvad — best for maximum anonymity

Mullvad takes a different approach entirely: no email address is required to sign up, and you can pay anonymously. It's less polished than the others on this list, but for anyone whose priority is minimizing the personal information tied to their account, it's a standout.

Heads up: pricing, server counts, and available plans change often for every VPN provider. Always check the current details on the provider's own site before subscribing.

Free VPNs vs. paid VPNs: is the upgrade worth it

Free VPNs aren't automatically a scam, but most operate under real constraints that paid options don't have. Running a global server network costs money, and if you're not paying for the service, that cost usually gets recovered somewhere else, whether that's data caps, slower speeds, ads, or in the worst cases, selling anonymized browsing data to third parties.

A genuinely free tier from a company that also sells a paid product, like ProtonVPN's, tends to be more trustworthy than a VPN that's entirely free with no clear business model. If you're only using a VPN occasionally, on public Wi-Fi, a reputable free tier can be enough. If you're using one daily for streaming, work, or privacy, the upgrade to paid is generally worth it.

VPNs by region: US, UK, Canada, and Australia

Where you're located changes what actually matters most in a VPN.

United States

US-based readers are usually looking for two things: keeping their internet provider from tracking browsing habits, and accessing streaming libraries or sports coverage not available in their state or region. Server count within the US matters here, since more local servers generally means better speeds.

United Kingdom

For UK users, VPNs are commonly used to securely access services like BBC iPlayer while traveling abroad, since these platforms restrict access based on location. A VPN with strong, well-maintained UK servers is the priority here, since streaming platforms actively work to detect and block VPN traffic.

Canada

Canadian users frequently run into regional blackouts for live sports, along with a smaller domestic streaming catalog compared to the US. A VPN with reliable US server access is often just as important as Canadian coverage for this reason.

Australia

Australia's geographic isolation means latency to overseas servers can be a real factor, so a VPN with fast, well-located Asia-Pacific servers matters more here than in most other regions. It's also worth checking a provider's stance on Australia's data retention laws when picking one for genuine privacy use.

Server location and count directly affect both speed and what regional content you can access.

Common VPN mistakes worth avoiding

  • Assuming a VPN makes you fully anonymous. It hides your traffic from your provider and the sites you visit, but it doesn't erase every other way you can be tracked, like account logins or browser fingerprinting.
  • Leaving the kill switch off. This feature cuts your internet if the VPN connection drops, preventing your real traffic from leaking out unprotected. Most people never turn it on.
  • Picking based on price alone. The cheapest option is sometimes cheap because it's cutting corners on server infrastructure or, worse, its privacy practices.
  • Never checking for DNS leaks. A properly configured VPN should route your DNS requests through the encrypted tunnel too. Several free tools online let you check this in under a minute.

Frequently asked questions

Do I actually need a VPN in 2026?

If you use public Wi-Fi, want to keep your browsing private from your internet provider, or want to access streaming content from another region, a VPN is genuinely useful. If none of that applies to you, it's a nice-to-have rather than a necessity.

Are free VPNs safe to use?

Some are fine for occasional light use, but many are slower, cap your data, or fund themselves by logging and selling browsing data, which defeats the purpose of using one at all.

Will a VPN slow down my internet?

Some speed loss is normal since your traffic is routed through an extra server, but well-optimized paid VPNs keep this minimal for everyday browsing and streaming.

Is it legal to use a VPN?

In the US, UK, Canada, and Australia, using a VPN is legal. What you do while connected to one still needs to follow the law and the terms of service of the sites you use.

The bottom line

Not every VPN on the market is worth paying for, but a good one is worth the ten or so dollars a month for what it protects you from, especially if you're regularly on public Wi-Fi or care about keeping your browsing data away from your provider. Pick based on your actual use case: NordVPN if you want a dependable default, ExpressVPN if streaming access is your priority, ProtonVPN or Mullvad if privacy is the main driver, and Surfshark if you're covering a household of devices on a budget.
